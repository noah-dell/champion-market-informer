Market Commentary
Champion Steel Works analysis

Sheet kept climbing: Nucor lifted its HRC CSP to $1,180/ton for the week of Aug 24 (+$10), a fourth straight increase, and SMU's HR average rose to $1,200/ton on Aug 27 (range $1,180–$1,220). Plate tightened further — SMU's plate average is now $1,395/ton ($1,360–$1,430), up $60 from the Jul 7 print, and SSAB Americas sent a letter Aug 25 seeking at least another $40/ton. On beams, no W-shape mill letter from Nucor-Yamato, Gerdau or SDI has been confirmed since the May round, so the level is restated at ~$2,604/ton (~+13% YoY). Correction: the Aug 1 and Aug 17 editions carried a ~$72/ton "August shipments" beam hike that traced to a 2008 trade report, not a 2026 letter — that $72 has been backed out. Rebar holds at its last verified level pending a fresh quote.

Supply-side: AISI capacity utilization firmed to 80.0% for the week ending Aug 22 (1.848M net tons, +4.8% YoY), up from 79.4% the prior week and back above the 80% tightness threshold. SMU puts production times near multi-year highs — HRC just over 6.5 weeks, plate just under 7.5, coated around 8 — roughly three to four weeks longer than last summer. Buyers at SMU's Steel Summit this week reported lean inventories and expect prices to keep rising near-term.

Steel bar joists (BLS WPU1074051, rebased Jan '24=100) are at 96.8 for July, +17.6% YoY. This corrects a base-year error in prior editions — the series actually shows a sharp 2024 decline followed by a 2025–26 recovery, not a steady climb.

Steel deck tracks HRC as a proxy and continues to firm. Composite floor deck (20 ga) runs $3.85–$5.40/SF; roof deck (22 ga) at $3.10–$4.60/SF — ranges unchanged this cycle pending a fresh quote, though the HRC-proxy index is up ~46% YoY.

Outlook: Tariffs, utilization back above 80%, multi-year-high lead times and data-center demand all point to firm-to-higher sheet and plate pricing into September. Beams are the one line where the market has gone quiet — treat the current level as a floor and re-check for mill letters before quoting. Keep escalation clauses on plate and structural work.

ABI Report — July 2026
46.6 down from 47.3 in June — softening resumes after a one-month bounce
July's 46.6 reading (released Aug 19) gave back most of June's gain, extending the downturn to roughly three and a half years — the longest in the index's history. The mix was split: project inquiries rose again (52.6) but the value of newly signed design contracts fell further to 47.2 after nearly reaching growth in June. By region the South led at 48.7, followed by the West (47.8) and Midwest (46.7), with the Northeast weakest for a second straight month at 44.8. By sector, multifamily residential led at 48.4, ahead of institutional (47.4) and commercial/industrial (46.7) — the latter has not posted billings growth since July 2022. AIA chief economist Richard Branch flagged high oil prices and the risk of higher rates in the back half of the year as added pressure on developers. The ABI leads nonresidential construction activity by 9–12 months, pointing to a slow, uneven path into 2027.
