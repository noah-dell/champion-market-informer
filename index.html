Market Commentary
Champion Steel Works analysis

Structural steel prices continue their relentless climb — HRC hit $1,045/ton (Nucor CSP, Apr 13), the latest in a long string of weekly price increases and now at the highest level since early 2024. Beams jumped to ~$2,384/ton after Nucor-Yamato and Gerdau NA raised beam and piling prices $40/st in early April 2026 — a clear reversal from the Jan '26 softness Gordian had flagged. Plate reached ~$1,200/ton (SMU, Apr 7), with Algoma announcing an additional $60/st increase. On April 2, sweeping Section 232 tariff changes applied tariffs to the full customs value of imported steel, aluminum, and copper, with tiered rates up to 50%.

Steel bar joists (BLS PPI series WPU1074051, Feb '26 index 116.8 rebased) have tracked the broader structural steel trend, climbing steadily through 2025 and into 2026. Lead times are 6–10 weeks for standard K-series under 25 tons and 8–14 weeks for larger projects; long-span LH/DLH-series at 12–16 weeks. Galvanized joists require additional lead time. Expect further pressure as HRC-driven input costs flow through.

Steel deck pricing tracks HRC closely and is trending upward. Composite floor deck (1.5" type B, 20 ga) is running $3.75–$5.25/SF installed; roof deck (1.5" type B/F, 22 ga) at $3.00–$4.50/SF. Raw material costs for metal roof decking run $3.00–$7.00/SF. Quotes are typically valid 30 days — escalation clauses are essential in the current environment.

Outlook: HRC has breached $1,000 and shows no signs of retreating. The April 2 tariff expansion to full customs value is adding further upward pressure. Mill lead times remain extended and spot availability is limited. SMU sources expect continued incremental sheet price gains through Q2 2026. Buyers should lock in pricing where possible and build escalation clauses into all new contracts.

ABI Report — February 2026
49.4 up from 43.8 in January
Business conditions at architecture firms may be stabilizing. Inquiries rebounded after a January dip, and while newly signed design contracts continued to decline, the pace slowed significantly. However, billings have been sub-50 for 38 of the last 41 months — this is a potential inflection point, not a confirmed recovery. The South held flat for a second consecutive month while the Northeast was hit by winter storms. The ABI leads nonresidential construction activity by 9–12 months.
